# Divinity accounting — what you actually spend

**Status:** RULED 2026-08-11 (the prayer cycle) · the two-quantity split below is my
reading of it and needs one confirmation (**A-01**).
Answers the sharpest catch in the 2026-08-11 external review (its §26).

---

## 1. The problem

The rule said **"1 divinity = 1 being who reveres you."** Read literally, that makes
divinity *identical to* a population — and then divinity is also spent on advancement, on
kingdom growth, and held as leverage against a patron. The reviewer put the objection
exactly right:

> *"If 500 reverent villagers = 500 divinity and I spend 100 divinity on a personal
> upgrade — what happened to the hundred people? Did I consume their faith? Did they die?"*

A population, a political standing, a spendable currency and an advancement resource are
four different economic objects. One number cannot be all of them without the fiction
explaining the accounting.

---

## 2. The ruling

> **Followers give you divinity every day, through prayer.** Fifty followers is roughly
> fifty divinity per session. **Gaining a follower counts as having been prayed to once**,
> so acquisition pays immediately. **Prayer needs no ritual** — thinking of you fondly or
> reverently is prayer.

**Divinity is not the people. Divinity is what the people produce.**

---

## 3. The two quantities

The confusion dissolves once the single word is split in two. ⟨**A-01** — confirm the split
and the names⟩

| | **STANDING** | **DIVINITY** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Your follower base — every named being who reveres you, plus everything bound beneath them | The **accumulated prayer income** those followers have produced |
| Unit | One named being = 1 | One prayer = 1 |
| Changes when | Followers are **gained, lost, killed, or defect** | You **earn** it each cycle, and **spend** it |
| Spending it | **Never spends standing** | Spends freely |
| Compared against | Other gods, up the vassalage graph | Your own outgoings |

> **Spending divinity does nothing to the people.** You are spending what they gave you,
> not them. A hundred divinity is two sessions of a fifty-strong congregation thinking well
> of you — not a hundred lives.

**Standing is the principal. Divinity is the yield.** That is the whole answer, and every
existing ruling survives it.

---

## 4. The prayer cycle

- **In the fiction:** daily.
- **At the table:** once per session — the unit the owner specified.
- **Rate:** 1 divinity per follower per cycle, modified by the follower's **weight** (a
  village is one named entry carrying many, per Q-23).
- **On acquisition:** gaining a follower counts as one prayer immediately. Mercy, rescue
  and a completed Goal all pay on the spot *and* start an annuity.
- **Prayer is passive.** No shrine, no rite, no words. A follower who thinks of you warmly
  has paid. This matters — it means reverence cannot be *performed* on command, only
  earned and then received.
- **The patron's cut** (Q-04) is taken **from the income, not the base.** Your followers
  stay yours; a negotiated share of what they produce goes up the chain.

---

## 5. It makes the existing rulings sharper, not weaker

| Ruling | What it means now |
|---|---|
| **Q-05** — you may break a patron's contract only by out-holding them | You must accumulate **divinity equal to your patron's standing**. A minor god with 50 standing costs 50; Odin's standing is his entire pyramid, so his contract is functionally unbreakable. **The bidding trade is now arithmetic**, not vibes |
| **Q-32** — a failed double-or-nothing burns earned reverence | It removes **followers**, not balance. That is far harsher than losing savings: you lose the annuity as well as the money. Correctly brutal for a declared bet |
| **Q-25** — a spared enemy can revere you | Pays **+1 immediately** and **+1 every cycle after**. Mercy isn't just the greedy play, it's the *compounding* play |
| **Q-53** — stability flows down only if the god allows | A god routes **income** downward. Withholding starves followers without reducing their number — until they leave, which is when standing finally drops |
| **Q-12** — unspent divinity sets your standing in the pantheon | You arrive with your **balance**, having built your **base**. Two different achievements, scored separately |
| **F-02** — divinity buys kingdom growth | You spend **yield** to grow **principal**. The only compounding use of the currency, which is exactly why it competes with everything else |
| **Q-64** — Vermilia rules a capital and is poor | Her city's prayer goes to seven queens who never existed. **Enormous standing, none of it hers, so no income.** The mechanism is now precise |

### Cinnabrus, exactly

Under this model his ledger is not zero and never was:

```
   STANDING:   1        (Vermilia — the only follower his plague did not kill)
   INCOME:     1 / cycle (she has prayed onto his tongue every day for 170 years)
   BALANCE:    ~62,000  (170 years of a single believer, and nothing to spend it on)
```

He is not a dead god. He is a god with **one follower, a trickle of income, and a
century's savings he cannot use** — because using divinity requires acting, and Beelzebub
took the organ he acts with. **The foreclosure is that he can bank but not spend, and can
never grow the base, because growing it requires answering someone.**

That is a better version of the character than the one we had this morning, and it came
out of the accounting rather than being written.

---

## 6. What this fixes downstream

- **§17 can now be written.** The audience chapter needs rates, and this supplies the base
  one: a Follower is worth 1 per cycle, weighted. Viewers stay abstract; the conversion
  from spectacle to a *named* follower is the only number left to tune.
- **The kingdom tree has real maths.** Population → standing → income → investment →
  population. Fields raise the ceiling on **standing**; Temples raise the **share of
  standing that is actually yours**; neglect drops standing to an absolute floor (F-14).
- **A GM can run it.** Standing is a list of names on a page. Divinity is a number that
  goes up once a session and down when you spend. Both are trackable on paper, which the
  review flagged as a live concern for the whole system.
