# Part A · The §8 review

## A.1 The Doorward *(packet §4.2 / §8.1)*

**Verdict: defensible — and misdiagnosed by its own author.** The "gotcha" framing assumed
killing the Doorward is a mistake. It isn't: the best ending (§6.1) requires Nullrot and
the Loong in the same room, and Nullrot chained forever is stasis — no cure ever exists.
The party's correct move is also correct in the campaign's own terms. **It is a trigger,
not a trap.**

The real question was never fairness but **recognition**: irony nobody perceives isn't
unfair, it's *unspent*, and recognition was route-gated behind Floor 3 Easy (the host
answering honestly). Recommended channels: the rival party that ran Easy holds the truth
as a tradeable (price it high); Corporation broadcast footage of the staircase exists by
definition.

> ⚠️ **Partially superseded during session:** the "no available clue" premise is deleted
> by the Doorward's warning speech (§B.3). The clue now exists, in the boss's own voice,
> at the moment of the choice. The encounter is upgraded from "the party cannot know" to
> **legible but insufficient** — the party hears the mechanism stated plainly and will
> read it as menace. Floor 3's recognition now has material to bite on: *"he told us."*

## A.2 The killable queen *(packet §4.1 / §8.2)*

**Verdict: feature.** The design thesis is that the cast destroys what it needs through
failure of recognition — Bex closed his own road. The unsignaled choice puts **the players
under the same law as the cast**; a warning label would have them playing a different game
than the characters. It is also not blind: by the decision point they know what she *is*
(a demon asking to be fed people); they cannot know what she *means*. That gradient is the
whole design.

Condition for defensibility: the deleted-ending world must be a story, not a stump —
**§6.4 already satisfies this.** One addition to the dead branch: 🟡 a scene where **Bex
learns that he closed the road** — the recognition beat that makes the Vermilia-dead
timeline worth playing rather than merely survivable.

> ⚠️ **Strengthened during session:** under the clan-fall origin (§B.6), history's shape
> repeats at Floor 1 Medium — a proxy-corrupted act, a clan child, a community that cannot
> read the stakes. The choice now has a signal for the first time, buried at maximum depth:
> **the clue is the entire pattern of history.** Fair the way the Doorward's speech is fair.
> And under the witness version (§B.7), *both* branches complete a circuit — see §B.7.

## A.3 The trade track *(packet §6.2 / §8.3)*

**Verdict: the number is real; the delivery surface is the accounting device.** Progress
0–3 is a good GM-side clock — keep it. What it cannot do is make purchased knowledge
*land*: reveals that arrive as line-items don't function as reveals.

Recommended instead of new machinery:

- **Rival parties in The Lounge** — the shared between-floors base has existed since v0.9
  and turns the track into scenes: boasts, drunken confessions, blackmail, on-camera
  betrayal the crowd pays for.
- **Broadcast footage as a purchasable object** — the show *would* make contestants watch
  the other routes' highlights. Converts "you chose and you'll never know" into "you chose,
  and you watched someone else live it."
- **Set 2 note:** the architecture already merges parties onto shared continents at
  Floor 4. A coherent reading exists where Set 1's knowledge isolation is deliberate
  capital spent when parties meet — i.e. §6.1 is an *early-Set-2* ending and the packet's
  open question "does Floor 3 converge the routes" resolves as **no — Floor 4 does**.

> Extended during session by 🔒 **rumors** and 🔒 **racing** (§B.1, §B.2). Rumors solve
> *awareness*, not *comprehension* — they cannot carry the load-bearing chain (mixture,
> mask, tongue) and complement rather than replace the channels above.

## A.4 Bex *(packet §5 / §8.4)*

**Verdict: not over-redeemed — the redemption test is gated in play even though it is
ungated in text.** A scaling cure only exists via the §6.1 path, so in most campaigns the
test can never be administered and Bex dies (or wins) as the child-burner. The text doesn't
redeem him; the players can *build* the thing that redeems him, and only in the branch
where redemption is materially possible. In the Vermilia-dead branch his sacrifice succeeds
and "the party carries it" — a monkey's paw, not absolution. The ending-space is honest.

Residual risk is register, not structure: §5's sympathy is design-doc voice. Guard: **Bex
never explains himself unprompted** — the apologia is extractable, never offered. His
ruled voice (§B.4) already satisfies this: *apology without confession*.

**The 170-year problem (§8.7a):** 🟡 patch using only ruled facts — the sacrifice-cure is
not something Bex always knew; it is something he **excavated** from fragments of
Cinnabrus's interrupted research, on the same clock as the demons combing the desert.
Everyone in Set 1 is digging up a dead god's homework.

## A.5 "No villains" *(packet §5 / §8.5)*

**Verdict: structure — conditionally.** The climax must be *contested*, and the contest
must come from the sympathetic cast's own damage, not a smuggled villain. Two resistances
are already available:

- **Vermilia's earned paranoia** — a person who spent two centuries and a civilization on
  the revival does not hand the final act to tourists on their word. The most aligned
  character as the final obstacle.
- **Beelzebub as architecture** — "a policy, not a character" is right, but a policy can
  *act*: the seals, the wards, the tongue's prison are Beelzebub present without appearing.
  **Let the final dungeon be the villain** — a security system built by someone the party
  never meets. Mechanics-as-story at its purest.

## A.6 Information architecture *(packet §8.6)*

**Verdict: the load-bearing facts flow almost entirely through the Easy column** (mural →
Easy; honest host → Easy F3; Nullrot's self-knowledge → Easy F3). Medium learns *who*;
Hard learns *where and what it costs*; Easy learns *what is actually happening*. A
Medium-only party can finish Floor 3 never having heard of the mask.

- If intentional — routes as difficulty of *understanding*, not just survival — it is
  genuinely good: 🔴 **write it down as a rule**, because it is currently an
  accident-shaped feature.
- If not: rebalance one comprehension channel into Hard. The Loong is the obvious carrier —
  it has investigated for 170 years, knows the most, and says the least.
- Either way: **the audience knows everything.** Betting odds shifting, replays, crowd
  reaction to the chained man — Exposure means the show *reacts*, and reaction is
  information. This one asset answers §8.1, §8.3 and §8.6 simultaneously; exhaust it
  before adding machinery.

## A.7 Contradictions *(packet §8.7)*

| # | Contradiction | Resolution | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bex's 170 years** to find one human | The requirement itself is excavated knowledge (§A.4) | 🟡 |
| 2 | **Why Nullrot needs the mask** if he can act through it | The cure ingredient is *the champion's flesh* — a mask has no flesh. Mask-riding buys agency; only completed conversion yields a body whose **substance is medicine**. He doesn't need the mask to act; he needs it to become *matter*. (Why conversion rebuilds the body; why the halo appears only once embodied; why killing him ends plague and cure together.) Deepened by §B.5: the mask is also Cinnabrus's engineered **bypass of the clan-death law** | ⚙️ |
| 3 | **Found by review, not flagged in packet:** §4.2 Medium rules "a demon is not killed by killing its body" while §4.1 kills Vermilia permanently by burning a house | **Resolved by the clan law (§B.5):** a demon dies when its clan dies; her clan is a population of one; *her body is her clan*. Not an exception to the rule — the rule proving how alone she is | 🔒 |

## A.8 The §6.3 proposals *(packet §8.8)*

**Both blessed.** The Loong's blood-claim is the best single idea in the packet —
Beelzebub's seal as a legal system with a jurisdiction gap, and the gap is *grievance*. It
converts the Loong from resource to actor: the campaign's most passive load-bearing
character gets the one door only it can open, standing on the prison of its ancestor's
killer, choosing to free him *because freeing him is the cure*.

Two cautions:

1. **The catalyst must cost.** If the first dose of the only cure ever made goes to a dead
   god's release while someone visible is dying of the plague in the same city, the
   catalyst is a decision instead of a ceremony.
2. **Collapse the requirements, not the scenes.** "Three routes, one room" concentrates
   six payoffs (cure, liberation, tongue, Vermilia's answer, Bex's test, Beelzebub's
   leverage) into one scene, which will play as ritual. Three beats, one act: cure made
   (with the hunt crashing it) → prison opened → revival.

> The finale gained a seventh payoff during the session — the verdict problem (§B.8) —
> which makes the beats-not-room structure *more* necessary, not less.

# Part B · Session changelog (2026-08-24)

Everything below post-dates the packet. Origin marked: **[owner]** stated by the owner,
**[review]** proposed or derived in review.

## B.1 🔒 The rumor network — [owner]

Players hear degraded rumors of the other routes' events: a 2.5-meter woman brought to a
village as an ill omen; commotion at the sealed ruins where everyone who went got sick and
died; a masked murderer in the slums.

- ⚙️ The rumors are wrong in *instructive* ways — the murderer rumor keeps the killing and
  loses the curing, which is Nullrot's tragedy compressed into how gossip works. The
  misrecognition engine running in the delivery channel.
- ⚙️ The 70/100-year skips build degradation into the fiction for free — old events arrive
  as legend.
- **Boundary:** rumors deliver *awareness*, never *comprehension*. The load-bearing chain
  (mixture, mask, tongue) still needs the Lounge / broadcast / trade channels.

## B.2 🔒 Rival parties race to decisions — [owner]

Rivals don't just trade information; they can race the party to decision points.

- 🔴 **OPEN — must be ruled:** can rivals resolve *campaign-defining* branch points
  (the queen, the Doorward) offscreen? Two defensible games: (a) rivals may **threaten**
  such branch points but resolution stays within player reach, or (b) offscreen
  resolutions are allowed but must always be **discoverable and answerable**. Undecided,
  the design will drift between them.

## B.3 🔒 The Doorward speaks — [owner]

The Doorward warns intruders aloud, e.g.: *"Beware, fools who seek this grave… My hunger
is sated here, feasting on this man. Do not make me turn it to you."* Area dressing:
glowing dust and similar cursed-gold signs attracting treasure hunters.

- 🔒 **Wording: "deception," not "illusion"** — [owner, on review flag]. In a system where
  actions auto-succeed when requirements are met, "illusion" is a capability claim that
  owes the players a resistance mechanic; "deception" lives in fiction and mints nothing.
- The mask's deception is that the host is **genuinely** a real, suffering, honest old man
  who wants out — every word he says can be true, and freeing him is exactly what the
  thing inside needs. **The truest voice in the room is the lure.** No false face
  required; the trap is that suffering is pitiable. (Bonus layer: even the Doorward
  slightly misreads what it warns about, attributing cunning where there is only
  situation.) — 🟡 framing [review]
- **Process rule this surfaced (🟡):** dialogue drafts get audited for fact-minting the
  same as design text — at the table, spoken lines *are* canon.

## B.4 🔒 Bex's voice — [owner]

Southern-sounding, polite, determined. *"Apologies, travellers. It is by no fault of your
own you chose wrong. And it is by no fault of your own that your downfall happens today."*

- ⚙️ This is **apology without confession** — he expresses condolence and never explains
  necessity, which satisfies the §A.4 guard natively. A demon who volunteers his reasons
  is asking for absolution; this one only offers condolences.
- 🔴 **OPEN — decide deliberately:** a genteel, unfailingly polite southern-accented man
  who owns a farm of human beings summons a specific historical image. Players will hear
  it either way. As deliberate resonance (perfect civility wrapped around ownership) it is
  potent and fits him; as an accident it will read as one.

## B.5 🔒 The clan law — [owner]

**Demons are a race born to enforce rules of nature.** Each clan embodies a thing that
exists in reality; as the thing grows, the clan grows (clan-level, not individual).

Ruled mechanics:

1. **A demon dies only when its clan dies.** (Resolves contradiction §A.7-3: Vermilia,
   last of her clan, is killable in the body — her body *is* her clan.)
2. **Rebirth runs through clan bloodline.** A dead demon can be reborn only to parents of
   its clan. Vermilia can restore the line by marrying and continuing the bloodline; a
   dead clan is unrecoverable.
3. **Nullrot sits outside the cycle.** Sealed in the mask; wearing it is **possession, not
   birth**. While sealed he cannot be reborn normally and **cannot sire clan members**
   (possession of a body is not clan flesh). **Destroying the mask returns him to the
   cycle** — which is exactly what Beelzebub is trying to avoid. *Sealing the mask is key.*
4. **Nullrot is Divine Wrath clan.**
5. **The Divine Wrath clan** embodies karma: *natural order will reward or punish you.*
   Closest of all clans to divine power; large in the age of gods, which is why a newly
   arisen god like Cinnabrus plausibly held a living congregation. The clan being killed
   by wrath's own instrument is intentional irony.
6. **The Corporation/Casino rules causality in this setting** — which is *why* the karma
   clan is scarce: spectacle replaced consequence as the operating law.

Derived consequences — ⚙️ unless vetoed:

- **The dynasty is literal machinery.** The capital's queens, "mother to daughter, all of
  them her," are a rebirth engine disguised as a dynasty — a last-of-clan demon building a
  bloodline is building a life raft. (Same packet sentence, twice the weight.)
- **The mask is an engineered bypass of the clan-death law.** §2's sequence: the plague
  eats the clan, *then* Cinnabrus blesses the mask. He had just watched clan-death nearly
  delete everything he was, and built his champion a return ticket that doesn't depend on
  the clan surviving. (Second-order answer to old §8.7b.)
- **Beelzebub seals rather than destroys because destruction is release** — the phylactery
  tradition inverted: every lich story says destroy the vessel; here destroying the vessel
  frees the monster. Beelzebub holds the only real prison shut.
- **In the Vermilia-dead branch, the prison becomes the life raft.** Clan dead in the
  cycle + Nullrot exempt via the mask = last wrath demon alive on a technicality; mask
  destruction after her death is an **execution** (return to a cycle with no one to birth
  him). Beelzebub ends up passively preserving the champion he removed. *Nobody chose
  this; it fell out of the rules* — the sign the ruling is load-bearing.
- **Nullrot cannot be permanently killed while the last believer lives.** Killing embodied
  Nullrot with the mask intact resets the loop (someone wears him back). Permanent death
  requires mask destruction → requires the clan dead → deletes the good ending. Faith
  keeps the champion functionally immortal — zero god-sentences required. **§6.4's
  kill-Nullrot ending is therefore era-scoped, not permanent.**
- **Time-skips absorb rebirth latency.** Bex's body-death at F2 "not taking" works at
  campaign timescale because rebirth-through-birth costs decades and the +100 years
  provides them.

Open nodes the law created:

- 🔴 **The oath-ignoring material that cuts the mask** is now the most consequential object
  in the setting — simultaneously Beelzebub's nightmare, possibly the host's freedom,
  possibly Nullrot's death *or* rebirth depending on who is alive. Promote from flavor
  note to canon entry.
- 🔴 **Clan inheritance specifics** — clan follows which parent, and what the children are.
  Note the consequence: if F3-era Vermilia has a bloodline, the killable-queen stake may
  only fully hold at Floor 1 (arguably correct — the irreversible choice belongs on
  Floor 1 — but decide it, don't discover it).
- 🔴 **Which clans are booming** under the Corporation's causality (population as
  metaphysical barometer) — likely Set 2 material, but it determines which demons exist
  by Floor 4. Related: the Corporation's in-fiction *age* is now canon-relevant.
- ⚠️ **Guard:** domain-decline must not become an unlabeled third hunger. The
  two-affliction confusion engine (plague vs. hunger) is precision machinery; fold any
  clan-starvation effect into the hunger Vermilia already has, or keep it out.
- 🟡 **Firewall handling of the wrath→benevolence chain** ("no one fears the gods → no one
  appeals, no one suspects Beelzebub; benevolence dies with wrath" — [owner]): the
  sociological surface is v1-speakable (a land that stopped praying and can't remember
  why — abandoned shrines, oaths that no longer bind, despair without the discipline fear
  used to buy, no government to dictate order); the theology lives on the Cinnabrus shelf,
  uncoverable and never required. Same method as Cinnabrus himself: symptom on the
  surface, diagnosis in the basement, theology in the other edition.

## B.6 🔒 The fall of the clan — deep-layer origin — [owner]

**Replaces the potency explanation at the deep layer** (potency was never the killer;
doubt was the vector). The surface truth — "his weapon ate his congregation" — survives as
what the mural-writers believed, what v1 parties can find, and **what Cinnabrus himself
believes**: he watched his plague take a clan child, never knew why, and may have died
thinking his weapon betrayed him. The packet's "mechanical, not moral" becomes the
in-fiction misunderstanding.

The chain:

1. One clanfolk — a child — is **enticed by Beelzebub**. Routine corruption, a small act
   (🟡 guard: keep it small and Beelzebub's full knowledge *retrospective* — the moment he
   planned the fall he's a nemesis, and §2's "scavenger, not a nemesis" is ruled canon.
   The most Beelzebub version: he profited enormously from something he barely did, and
   he's the only one who knows how little he did).
2. The plague releases; **the karmic cycle takes the corrupted child** — he sickens and
   crystallizes. *This is the one non-coincidence in the story: karma functioning exactly
   as specified.* The only coincidence is the intersection — Beelzebub's poaching sitting
   in the plague's path.
3. The clan reads it as their god killing a child for no reason. **Faith dwindles; doubt
   is the vector; the plague takes them.** What killed the clan was that **justice,
   illegible from the outside, is indistinguishable from cruelty.** The wrath clan died
   of unreadable wrath.
4. ⚙️ **Thesis weld:** a world where the Corporation rules causality is a world where
   consequence is *permanently* illegible — the clan's death is the proof-of-concept for
   the disease the Corporation later institutionalized. Origin story and metaphysical
   villain now share a thesis.
5. ⚙️ **The F1 rhyme:** prehistory's shape (proxy-corrupted act → clan child → community
   that cannot read the death → clan dies) repeats at Floor 1 Medium (rival demon working
   through human proxies → house burning with the last clan child inside → bystanders who
   cannot read the stakes). In a karma setting, repetition of shape is what unresolved
   karma *does* — and it is the killable queen's first signal (§A.2).

**Knowledge holders:** Beelzebub — full picture. Cinnabrus — none. Vermilia — see B.7.

- 🟡 **Craft note on the irony:** harder version available than "by mistake" — the plague
  didn't malfunction; it functioned *as wrath*, and wrath doesn't exempt its own. Whether
  the clan deserved it is a question the setting never answers (see §B.9 guard).

## B.7 🔒 Vermilia, the witness — [owner]

**Supersedes** the interim "purest believer" reading (under which the plague took the
guilty and doubting, certifying the sole survivor's faith). **Do not keep both survival
explanations — they contradict.** The trade, priced: "last true believer" is exchanged for
"the believer whose faith was never actually faith" — she couldn't doubt because she
*knew*, and she can no longer tell the difference herself.

Ruled facts:

- She **witnessed the child's corruption** — the deed. She knew why he got sick and what
  he had done.
- 🔒 **AMENDED (rev 2):** the first silence was an act of **intended bravery** — she
  helped the child hide his betrayal, knowingly risking her god's wrath to protect him.
  Fear of scrutiny and isolation motivates the *second* silence only, and the fear is
  grounded: by then the truth incriminated her as an accomplice. That is what brought her
  to the point where she's alone. She kept her faith *because* she was the sole witness.

> **Thematic seed (rev 2):** her core question is drawn from Mili's *"And Then Is Heard
> No More"* — whether someone who fully intended to be brave can rightly be called a
> coward. The question lives at the level of *intention*, which is exactly the layer the
> setting refuses to adjudicate (§B.9).
- Floor 1 Vermilia is an **incomplete character, searching for karma that never comes** —
  her encounter design must carry this (owner-flagged as needing design; direction in ⚙️
  below).

Derived — ⚙️ unless vetoed:

- **Two silences, one sin — now with a phase boundary (rev 2).** Silence one is courage:
  concealing a living child's sin, braced for the wrath it might incur. Silence two is
  where the courage curdles: the child is dead, the clan is dying of the misreading, and
  speaking would expose not only the truth but *her part in hiding it*. She crossed the
  boundary without ever making a new decision — she kept doing the brave thing past the
  point where it stopped being brave. The compounding stands: every funeral made speaking
  costlier, which made more funerals. **Her silence is what killed the clan** — she held
  the fact that would have kept its faith intact.
- **Thesis completion.** Bex closed his own road; Cinnabrus's weapon ate his congregation;
  Vermilia's silence destroyed the only people whose survival could have kept her from
  being alone. Every major figure in Set 1 now destroys what they need through the same
  failure of recognition. She was the last exception; the witness version enrolls her.
- **Her survival is her sentence.** She did the concealment *expecting to pay for it* —
  and the plague took everyone who doubted while skipping the accomplice who stood braced
  for the blow. "Searching for karma that never comes" is literal from day one: two
  hundred years of flinching at a strike that never lands, a karma demon denied her own
  verdict. Her sentence was to live — sole custodian of the truth, two centuries of
  hunger, digging alone. She isn't waiting for karma; she is *serving* it, and cannot
  recognize her own sentence. When the verdict wouldn't come to her, she spent two
  centuries excavating the judge.
- **The tongue doubles.** She didn't just dig up a god who can't speak — she dug up **a
  judge who cannot pronounce sentence.** Two hundred years of digging found the body and
  never the verdict.
- **Both F1 branches now complete circuits.** She is the clan child in the fire — hidden
  corruption, bystanders who can't read it. *Save her:* someone finally does for her what
  she failed to do for him. *Kill her:* karma arrives in the exact shape of her sin. The
  worst ending completes a circuit instead of merely deleting one.

Proposals:

- 🟡 **She witnessed the deed, not the dealer** — she did not see Beelzebub. Preserves him
  as sole holder of the full picture, banks "it was Beelzebub" as a late detonation, and
  avoids explaining two hundred years without a revenge plot.
- 🟡 **F1 performance: one wrong note.** Incompleteness must be playable in a rescue
  encounter — gratitude that lands like disappointment; a child in a fire who reads it as
  something *arriving* rather than something happening. Legible-but-insufficient, same
  grade as the Doorward's speech; plant at F1, detonate at F3.

## B.8 🔒 The verdict problem — Cinnabrus's bind — [owner + review]

⚙️ Derived from ruled facts (god at zero followers is deleted; she is the last; she is
guilty): **the wrath god cannot punish his last follower without annihilating himself.**
His options are mercy under duress of his own existence — which she can never trust was
real — or honest justice, which is suicide. Karma's final judgment, structurally
impossible to deliver cleanly.

🔒 **[owner]:** the god who killed all his people must *choose his death* to fulfil karma —
which is itself a karma upon him.

🟡 **The earned-karma reasoning (bank this — without it the beat is shape-rhyming):**
under the witness version Cinnabrus technically didn't sin; the plague worked as
specified. What he is punished for is **building an instrument of justice that couldn't
explain itself** — he delivered judgment illegibly, and his sentence is proportionate:
his own final judgment cannot be delivered legibly. The god of unreadable verdicts gets
an unreadable verdict.

🔴 **OPEN — the loophole that is probably the finale:** the bind holds only while she is
his *sole* follower. He died reaching for a cure that lifts the hunger from demons who
ask — and demons who ask are, functionally, congregation. Cure first → followers grow →
judging her stops being suicide. Therefore: **the only way this god can afford wrath is
to first perform benevolence.** Sequencing is in the players' hands — a party that
engineers the cure before the confession changes what the verdict can *mean* (judgment
under duress vs. judgment freely given; the party unknowingly decides which courtroom
convenes). Rule it deliberately; it will come up.

⚙️ **The tongue as theme:** Beelzebub cut out the judge's ability to pronounce sentence;
the revival restores his voice at precisely the moment the only sentence worth pronouncing
would cost him his existence. Two centuries mute — and the first true thing said to him is
a confession he cannot afford to answer. (This is the seventh finale payoff; see §A.8's
beats-not-room caution.)

⚙️ **The confession's precise question (rev 2):** not *"I did this"* but *"was I a
coward?"* — a question about **intention**, asked of the judge she spent two centuries
excavating, who structurally cannot pronounce. This is the finale's emotional shape.

## B.9 🟡 The closure guard — [review]

The karma web is approaching total closure — Bex, Cinnabrus, Vermilia, the clan, the
god's own verdict, every thread answering another. That is a strength until it becomes a
proof, where every event has a moral address and the setting feels like a solved equation.
**Leave something unanswered on purpose.** Natural candidate: **the clan child — and
(rev 2) Vermilia's question is the same case seen from the other side.** His fall and her
concealment are one event; whether *he* deserved what karma did to him and whether *she*
was a coward are the two faces of a single verdict the setting refuses to deliver. One
unadjudicable case, load-bearing from both directions. A karma system with exactly one
such case is a theme; one that explains everything is a spreadsheet.

# Part C · Process findings

## C.1 The delivery-channel column

The recurring failure across §8.1, §8.2 and §8.6 was never missing answers — it was that
the *truth layer* gets documented while the *delivery layer* lives in the owner's head
(the rumor network, the race, the Doorward's speech, and the clan law were all
load-bearing and all undocumented four days after the packet declared itself
self-contained). Fix: extend the ruled/proposed/open ledger with a second axis — **for
each load-bearing fact, list how it can reach a party, per route.** The moment "the
Doorward's feeding stalls the conversion" has a listed channel (his own speech), the
gotcha objection becomes unwritable.

## C.2 Versioning

The packet went stale four days after its own date stamp — design velocity outpaces
documentation cadence, and the review process is functioning as a *generator* (§8's open
questions provoked B.1, B.3 and B.5 within days). For a two-edition canon with a firewall,
lore needs version control: a changelog on the story doc, five minutes per idea, makes
