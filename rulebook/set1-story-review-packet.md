# Galactic Prime Time — Set 1 Story Review Packet

**Prepared 2026-08-20 · self-contained · for external review**

You are being handed the complete story design for **Floors 1–3 ("Set 1")** of a tabletop
RPG campaign, plus enough background to judge it without access to the rulebook or the
repository. Everything you need is in this document.

**What I want from you is in §8.** Read §1–§7 first.

---

# §1 · What the game is

**Galactic Prime Time** is a tabletop RPG. The premise: **aliens abduct humans (and
animals, and machines) and make them compete in a broadcast dungeon-crawl reality show.**
The in-fiction network is the **Corporation™**. The audience is a live mechanic — a
contestant earns **Exposure** for spectacle and loses standing for safe, passive play.

Relevant design facts, because they shape what the story is allowed to do:

| Fact | Consequence for story |
|---|---|
| **No to-hit rolls.** Actions auto-succeed when their requirements are met | Difficulty is *conditions, the clock, and being unqualified* — not whiffing. Encounters are puzzles of resource and position, so a boss can legitimately be unkillable-by-damage without feeling like GM cheating |
| **Combat runs on a 10-Moment Clock counting 10→1, ~5 seconds of fiction.** Actions overlap and never interrupt | "One Clock" is the unit for timers, escalations and countdowns throughout the story below |
| Bosses need **discoverable win conditions**, never raw damage races | Every Set 1 boss below has a *non-combat* solve. This is a house rule, not a stylistic preference |
| Damage resolves **per body part**, not against a pooled HP bar | Enemies are lists of parts. A "gate" part (a mask, a choir, a reservoir) can be the actual objective |
| Contestants are **abducted moderns** — a Russian cat, a Latin American dancer, a WWII veteran | The party is *tourists* in this world. Nobody in the party has any stake in the story below except the stakes they choose |

## 1.1 Floor and route structure

- **Floor 1** — a green forest.
- **Floor 2** — a great desert, **70 years later**.
- **Floor 3** — a grand capital, **100 years after that** (so ~170 years after F1).
- Each floor offers **three labelled routes: Easy, Medium, Hard.**
- 🔴 **A party picks ONE route per campaign.** The other two resolve offscreen. This is the
  single most important structural constraint in this document, and §8 asks about it.

Set 1 is Floors 1–3. Floors 4–6 (Set 2) merge parties onto shared continents and are
**not designed yet**.

## 1.2 Two editions, and a firewall

There are two versions of this game and **they must not be conflated**:

- **v1** — the edition currently being played. The **Corporation™** runs the show. There is
  no divine economy. The operative editorial test is: ***"any sentence that needs the word
  'god' does not belong in the book."***
- **v2** — a later "mythology edition" where the show is a table in a **Cosmic Casino**:
  gods wager on contestants, divinity is a currency, and bankruptcy is a real state a god
  can be in.

**The story below is the v1 telling.** A god *appears* in it, but only as **background a
thorough party can uncover** — never as the reason the cameras are on. Where a beat would
require the Casino to make sense, the v1 version has been given a physical equivalent
instead. §6.3 is the one place the editions genuinely diverge and it is marked.

# §2 · The prehistory (before any player sees anything)

```
 CINNABRUS, a plague demon, rises on the strength of his disease.
        │
 He KILLS A DRAGON with it ─────────────▶ the deed that makes his name.
        │
 The plague does not stop. It spreads.
 It kills EVERY ONE OF HIS OWN FOLLOWERS — except one.
        │
 Desperate, he turns to DRAGON'S BLOOD, and finds a method.
        │
 He BLESSES A MASK for his champion NULLROT — a reliquary, so that
 if the champion is killed, the champion can be worn back.
        │
 BEELZEBUB sends champions. Cinnabrus is put down before he can
 finish the cure. Beelzebub takes his domain and his title,
 CUTS OUT HIS TONGUE, and buries it separately, in its own prison.
        │
 And he SEALS THE MASK, because a champion is a return ticket.
        │
        ▼
 Buried under the forest. Mute. One follower left alive.
 A sealed mask, lying in a treasure room, waiting for someone to pick it up.
```

**The tragedy is mechanical, not moral.** Cinnabrus did not fall because he was wicked.
**His weapon ate his congregation.** He was very good at exactly one thing and it killed
everyone who believed in him. This is deliberately a story that needs no cosmology — which
is what lets it survive in v1.

**Beelzebub is a scavenger, not a nemesis.** He did not defeat a rival in a great war; he
arrived after the disaster and took the vacant title. He seals things rather than destroying
them, because destruction makes enemies and a shut door makes a dependent.

# §3 · The cast

| Who | What they are | What they want |
|---|---|---|
| **Cinnabrus** | The buried plague god. Mute — Beelzebub took the tongue | To finish the cure he was interrupted making, and to be answerable again |
| **Vermilia** | **The queen.** His first follower, and **the only one who survived his plague** | To revive him — so she can be cured of the **hunger** |
| **Nullrot** | **His champion.** An olden demon who owes Cinnabrus his life. Loyal to the god, not to the regent who replaced him | To complete the cure. He needs a body, and **his own reliquary mask** is how he returns |
| **The host** | The man who put the mask on at the staircase. **He is still in there** | Nothing, particularly. He **co-pilots.** Not for, not against — 170 years of this makes a person *different*, not loyal |
| **The Loong** | **Descendant of the dragon Cinnabrus killed.** A living blood-claim | To find the source of what took its city — and it has heard of the mask |
| **Bex** | A rival demon, working through humans | **The hunger lifted off demonkind** and Beelzebub's leash cut. His own cure is only the version he can *reach* |
| **Beelzebub** | The scavenger who won | To keep what he took — and to keep his followers *needing* him |

## 3.1 The two hungers, and the one cure

Two different afflictions run through Set 1, and they are constantly mistaken for each other
**by the characters**, which is the engine of most of the plot:

1. **The plague** — Cinnabrus's disease. It crystallises people. It is still leaking out of
   the ground under Floor 1, which is why Floor 2 is a desert.
2. **The hunger** — what being a demon costs. Demons must feed on people to keep existing.
   Beelzebub *could* lift it from his followers and does not, because **a cured follower is
   a free one.**

| | Holds the plague at bay | Soothes demonic hunger | **Actually cures** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nullrot** (the champion's flesh) | ✅ partially — he stalls what he leaks | ✖ | ✖ alone |
| **Loong blood** | ✅ temporarily | ✅ temporarily | ✖ alone |
| **Both together** | — | — | ✅ **yes** |

> **Neither is a cure. Together they are** — strong enough to *resist* the plague rather
> than merely delay it. This is exactly what Cinnabrus was reaching for when Beelzebub's
> champions stopped him.

**This is load-bearing.** It converts the "good ending" from a courtesy into a **necessity**:
the cure does not exist anywhere in the world until Nullrot and the Loong are in the same
room. Nobody can hand it to the party. It has to be *made*, by introducing two people.

**And Loong blood alone is a trap.** It sustains, so a captured Loong is not a murder — it
is **livestock**, bled indefinitely by demons who only need it to keep working. That is what
the demon hunt on the Hard route actually intends, whether or not it admits it.

## 3.2 The mask

| | |
|---|---|
| **Blessed by** | **Cinnabrus**, for his champion — *"if you are killed, you can be worn back."* A reliquary, not a weapon |
| **Sealed by** | **Beelzebub**, when he removed Cinnabrus. He did not destroy it — he *shut* it |
| **Therefore** | Everything the mask does to a host is **a sealed door being forced from the inside.** That is why it takes a body apart and rebuilds it. That is why it leaks plague. That is why it takes **seventy years** |

Three consequences worth flagging to a reviewer, because they are the parts I think are
strongest and would most like challenged:

- **The Floor 1 mural is not a prophecy.** It says *chain him*, and it is **a note left by
  people who knew what the object in that room was.**
- **The mask's invulnerability has a reason.** It resists ordinary harm because it is
  **blessed**; the one material in the game that cuts it is the one that ignores oaths.
- **The host survives, and that is load-bearing.** The seal is why the conversion never
  cleanly completed. **There are two of them in there.**

# §4 · The story, floor by floor

Read this as a **3×3 grid**. Each row is a floor; each column is a route. A party plays
**one column**. The columns are the same story seen from three angles, and none of the three
is sufficient on its own.

## 4.1 Floor 1 — the green forest

| Route | What the players see | What is actually happening |
|---|---|---|
| **Easy** | A man near a grand staircase descends with them, claims a treasure — **a mask** — and is possessed. A mural instructs them to **chain him to the wall.** The dungeon collapses on the way out | **The mask is Nullrot's own reliquary, sealed by Beelzebub.** The champion is forcing it open from the inside; forcing it is what dismantles and rebuilds the host. The "possession" is a *conversion in progress*, and **the chains hold it mid-conversion for seventy years** |
| **Medium** | NPCs are burning a house with **a girl** inside. Save her and she is a demon; she asks to be **fed**, and grants a **demonic brand** | **The girl is VERMILIA**, hiding among humans. **The arsonists' leader is BEX**, a rival demon burning out a rival claimant by proxy — **and he does not know she is the last follower alive** |
| **Hard** | A **moving city** on giant stairs, guarded by a **Loong Kin**. The citizens have **crystallised**. Convince it the city is gone | This is Cinnabrus's plague still leaking out of the ground. The Loong is the **dragon's descendant**, guarding what is left of a people it could not save |

> ⚠️ **The forest is the last of the green.** The thing buried underneath is still leaking,
> which is why Floor 2 is a desert. That is the setting telling the players something 70
> years before they can act on it.

### 🔴 The killable queen — the campaign's heaviest consequence

Cinnabrus's followers number **exactly one**. If Vermilia dies, **he hits absolute zero, and
a god at zero cannot be prayed back.**

- **Kill her at Floor 1** → **Cinnabrus is permanently deleted.** The best ending is gone
  from the campaign, on the first floor, before anyone knows what they did.
- **And Bex helped.** He wanted a rival gone. **He destroyed the one road he needed** — which
  is exactly why, 170 years later, he is farming humans for a sacrifice. *The easy road was
  closed and he closed it.*

The Medium route's F1 encounter presents "a mob is burning a house with a child in it" and
lets the party do the obvious thing, or not, with **no signal** that a campaign-length
outcome hangs on it. §8 asks whether that is defensible.

## 4.2 Floor 2 — the great desert, +70 years

| Route | What the players see | What is actually happening |
|---|---|---|
| **Easy** | The ruined stair; the man still chained, ancient, unpossessed. **A demon blocks the exit.** Kill it, free him | **The Doorward is sick and feeding.** It has drunk the leak off the chained man for seventy years — and *that drain is what stalled the conversion.* **Kill it and the seal gives. Nullrot walks out, with the man still inside** |
| **Medium** | The girl is a **queen**. She sends them to assassinate a **rival demon** who helped humans and wants her throne | It is **Bex**. They may kill him. **It does not take** — a demon is not killed by killing its body |
| **Hard** | The Loong, **hurt and hunted**, escorted to a village where it finds purpose | **It is on an errand.** Seventy years crossing the desert hunting the source of the plague — and out here **it heard of the mask.** It is right, and not in the way it hopes |

**The Doorward is the design piece I would most like a second opinion on.** It is a boss the
party is *correct* to kill, on all the evidence they hold, and killing it releases the thing
that will define Floor 3. There is no clue available at Floor 2 that would let a careful
party do better. It is not noble; it is a sick creature protecting its food supply. **Nothing
in the encounter is a trick — the party simply cannot know.**

**The demons are combing the desert** for Cinnabrus's grave. That is why F2 is a search, and
why demons are on all three routes.

## 4.3 Floor 3 — the grand capital, +100 more

**Vermilia searched a hundred years, found the grave, and built a capital on top of it** —
because a palace is the only building nobody digs under. **The capital is a two-century
excavation disguised as a civilisation.** She has ruled as a succession of queens, mother to
daughter, all of them her.

| Route | What the players see | What is actually happening |
|---|---|---|
| **Easy** | **Nullrot** spreads and cures a disease in the same street, on the same day. Fight or help | The champion, embodied at last, **finishing his god's interrupted cure.** He leaks because the mask leaks. He heals because he is *half* of the answer. The man from the staircase is still in there and **will answer questions honestly**, including about the Doorward |
| **Medium** | **Bex** runs a **human farm** and wants one specific human sacrificed to cure his demonic nature | The farm is **how the city's demons eat** — it is not a ritual site. The sacrifice is Bex's private hope riding on top of an operation with other reasons to exist. **He is also looking for the Loong** |
| **Hard** | The Loong hides in the capital, suppressing the plague; demons hunt it for its blood | **It followed the mask here.** The suppression is a side effect the capital mistook for a purpose — and **the hunt believes it holds a cure it has never had** |

**Under the palace: Cinnabrus, and the empty socket where his tongue was.** The tongue is
**not** down there with him. Beelzebub buried it apart, in its own prison, and **two
centuries of digging have found the god and never found the mouth.**

# §5 · The three antagonists, and why none of them is a villain

This is the thesis of the design and the thing most worth attacking.

**Nullrot** is a loyal champion finishing his god's work. He is *both* patient zero and the
antibody — not as a paradox but as a body: he carries a reservoir of the plague and sheds a
halo of cures. **Killing him ends the plague and ends the cure**, and the capital has a
century of people alive only because of the halo. He knows he is not a cure on his own. He
has been looking for a dragon for a hundred years.

**Bex** wants **the hunger lifted off every demon** and Beelzebub's leash cut — no more
farms, no more eating people to keep existing. **His own cure is simply the only version he
can reach**, so he takes it and pays what it costs. He burned a house with a child in it. He
has bred and bought humans for 170 years. His means are ugly, he knows it, and he does not
defend them. **The test that proves he means it: offer him a cure that scales, and he drops
the sacrifice on the spot**, even though it does nothing for him first.

**Vermilia** is the last living follower of a dead god, who has spent two hundred years and
an entire civilisation digging him up, so that she can stop being hungry. She is also the
child the party either saved or burned on Floor 1.

**Beelzebub** is the only one who is straightforwardly self-interested — and he never appears
in Set 1. He is a policy, not a character.

> **Every antagonist in Set 1 wants something reasonable and is reaching for it the only way
> they can see. The optimal ending is not mercy — it is introductions.**

# §6 · The endings

## 6.1 The path of least casualties

**Get the Loong out of the city · make the Loong and Nullrot meet · revive Cinnabrus.**

| Step | Why |
|---|---|
| **Loong + Nullrot meet** | **The cure only exists as a mixture.** This is the step that creates it, and it is the thing Cinnabrus was killed for attempting |
| **The Loong is freed** | It stops being a containment device it never volunteered to be — and stops being worth capturing |
| **Cinnabrus is revived** | Only possible while **Vermilia lives.** He finishes the cure, answers the follower who prayed for two centuries, and **lifts the hunger from demons who ask** |
| **Beelzebub loses his leverage** | He withholds the cure because *a cured follower is a free one.* A working cure ends that — **and Bex's leash comes off** |

🔴 **The problem:** this path requires knowledge from **all three routes**, and §1.1 gives a
party **one route per campaign.** See §8.

## 6.2 The current answer — rival parties and a trade track

Other countries' contestants run the other two routes offscreen. Each rival holds
**Progress 0–3** on its route, one step per floor; **+1 per floor the players clear** (they
are on the same clock); **±1** when the players help, hinder, or sell them something. At
Floor 3: **3 = resolved their way · 2 = resolved badly · 0–1 = unresolved.**

**What trades:** knowledge (what the mask is, what the blood does, who the girl was),
materials from bands the party never visited, and favours. Betrayal is always on the table
and the crowd pays for it.

**The reasoning:** letting one party run all three routes triples the content, flattens route
identity, and deletes the "you chose, and you will never know" pathos. Trading keeps the
choice while making the gap **recoverable at a price**. Without a number, offscreen parties
become whatever the GM needs that week; with one, the players can see the race.

## 6.3 🔒 The revival, and the one place the editions diverge

**The tongue is REQUIRED. There is no other route.** A god with no mouth cannot answer,
cannot be bargained with, and cannot be prayed back no matter how many followers he has.

| Step | v1 (this document) | v2 (the mythology edition) |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Find the tongue** | A physical object in a prison Beelzebub built. Finding it **unlocks interaction** with Cinnabrus — that is all it does, and it is everything | Same |
| **2 · Settle what is owed** | 🟡 **A catalyst, or breaking the prison by some means** — a physical release, because v1 has no ledger to pay | ⛔ **Pay his debt with the party's collected divinity.** A *table* settlement that **does not exist in v1** |

> **v2 buys a god out of bankruptcy. v1 digs him up.** Same tongue, same god, completely
> different verb. This is the firewall from §1.2 doing real work.

**🟡 Two proposals, not yet approved by the owner:**

1. **The v1 catalyst is the cure itself** — he died reaching for the mixture, so finishing it
   is the one offering that means anything to him, and the party is already carrying it if
   they took §6.1's path.
2. **The Loong is what opens the prison** — Beelzebub's seal answers gods and demons; it has
   no reply to **the blood-claim of the dragon Cinnabrus killed.** The descendant standing on
   it is a claim the seal cannot refuse.

**If both land, §6.1's three steps collapse into one act:** getting the Loong to the capital
creates the cure, frees the Loong, *and* opens the tongue. Three routes, one room.

## 6.4 The worse endings

- **Vermilia was killed at F1** → Cinnabrus is deleted. Nullrot's cure and Loong blood still
  exist as a mixture, but there is no patron to lift the hunger, the unbent demons stay
  rampaging, and Bex's sacrifice becomes the only cure anyone can reach. The Floor 3 crown is
  human and frightened rather than hers.
- **The party grants Bex's petition** — hand him the human. He becomes mortal, keeps his word,
  and the party carries it. Enormous Exposure. The crowd loves it and nobody else will.
- **The party kills Nullrot** — ends the plague and the cure together, and the capital finds
  out what the halo was doing about a week later.
- **The Loong is captured** — livestock, bled indefinitely, and every demon in the city gets
  a temporary reprieve forever.

# §7 · What is settled and what is not

**Ruled (owner-approved, treat as fixed):** the prehistory chain · the two hungers and the
combined cure · the mask's blessing and seal · the host co-piloting · Bex acting for
demonkind · Vermilia as the killable last follower · Bex as the rival demon across all three
floors · the tongue being required · the plague being one plague across all three routes ·
Nullrot as both source and antibody · the rival-party trade track.

**Proposed (mine, unapproved):** the v1 catalyst being the cure itself · the Loong's
blood-claim opening the tongue's prison.

**Open:** where the tongue's prison actually is · whether Floor 3 converges the routes.

# §8 · What I want from you

Please review as a **story and structure editor**, not a copy-editor. In rough priority:

1. **Is the Doorward defensible?** (§4.2 Easy) A boss the party is *correct* to kill, with no
   available clue, whose death causes Floor 3. Is that a great irony or an unfair gotcha?
   Does the answer change depending on whether the party ever finds out it was them?

2. **Is the killable queen defensible?** (§4.1) A first-floor choice with no signal that
   silently deletes the campaign's best ending. Note that the "wrong" move — killing a demon
   who is asking to be fed people — is the *ethically defensible* one. Is that a feature?

3. **The one-route-per-campaign problem.** (§6.1 vs §1.1) The best ending needs all three
   routes' knowledge. My answer is the trade track (§6.2). **Is that a real solution or an
   accounting device pretending to be one?** What would you do instead?

4. **Is Bex over-redeemed?** He burns a child, farms humans for 170 years, and I have written
   him as acting for his whole people. Does the "offer him a scaling cure and he drops the
   sacrifice" test actually earn that, or is it a cheat that lets the audience off?

5. **Is "no villains" a structure or an evasion?** (§5) Every antagonist is sympathetic and
   the optimal ending is introductions. Does that produce a satisfying climax, or does the
   campaign lack a thing to *push against*?

6. **The information architecture.** The party are abducted moderns with no stake. Almost all
   of this is discoverable only through GM-side reveals across ~9 sessions. **Is the story
   legible from the players' seats,** or is it a beautiful thing only the GM ever sees?

7. **Anything that contradicts itself.** Timelines, motives, who knows what and when. Two
   specific spots I am least confident about: **why Bex takes 170 years** to find one human,
   and **why Nullrot needs the mask at all** if he can act through it before the conversion
   completes.

8. **§6.3's two proposals** — bless, reject, or replace.

Where you disagree, say what you would do instead. Concrete alternatives are worth more to
me than a verdict.
