# Registration — beat sheet

The System's arrival, Chapters 3–5 of the opening arc. Markers: 🔒 author-ruled ·
⚖ proposed, unblessed · 🔴 open.

---

## 🔒 The governing rule — one sentence

> **THE SYSTEM TRANSCRIBES. IT DOES NOT UNDERSTAND.**

Everything the scene does comes out of that. It is a speech-to-text engine
pointed at a man's head: it renders whatever crosses his mind, phonetically,
with no comprehension and no ranking. So —

- *Surely this is a joke* → **SHIRLY THISSISAJOKE**. It heard sounds.
- *last year* in the **Sex** field → **"Not a date field."** It did not get the
  joke. It got a **type error**. The machine is a compiler, not a comedian, and
  it is technically correct.
- **Sponsor** → it cannot reason about sponsorship, so it retrieves the nearest
  matching token in memory. It finds the man who handed him the ticket.

⭐ **The rule is also the misdirection.** Three fields of visible literal-minded
failure teach the reader *"this thing is dumb"* — which is exactly the belief
required for them to misread the sponsor resolution. **The scene builds its own
alibi two fields early.**

## Phase 1 — THE GATE · Ch. 3

> **"Do you want to live?"**

- 🔒 He answers **yes**.
- ⚖ **Offer YES and NO, and make NO genuinely selectable.** A gate with one
  button is a joke; a gate where refusal works is a **contract**.
- He answers instantly, without reading. Nobody reads terms.
- ⭐ **This is the actual abduction.** Not the teleport — the teleport is
  delivery. He consents to *the game* believing he consents to *survival*,
  which is the Forsaken trick in one line of UI. He should not realise for a
  long time.

## Phase 2 — NAME · Ch. 3 · 🔒 the Shirly gag

| Beat | |
|---|---|
| Prompt | **NAME:** |
| His thought | *Surely this is a joke.* — **present**, ungated, he did not choose to send it |
| Field | **SHIRLY THISSISAJOKE** |
| His reply | 🔒 **"NO."** |
| ⚖ The recursion | It takes that too. **NAME: NO.** Once only — twice is a bit. |
| Resolution | Angry enough to think clearly, he gives it his actual name |

⭐⭐ **This is where the reader learns he is Marcus** — two and a half chapters
in, and **the show is what names him.** His identity enters the book as a form
field, corrected under duress. It also closes the Ch. 1 problem where the
broadcast printed *Daniel Krieg* under one photo and skipped the name under his.

⭐ And the correction is **his first act of agency in the entire novel.** He has
been dismissive, asleep, and dragged; the first thing he does under his own
steam is fix a form field. Funny and bleak in the same gesture.

## Phase 3 — CURRENT AGE · Ch. 3

- The straight man between two jokes. He answers correctly; it is the only
  field that goes right.
- ⚖ The barb is not a gag — it is that the system **approves**, and then
  populates a second value beside it that nobody asked for. Age is a **betting
  input**; this is a casino instrument. Approval is more unsettling than a joke.
- 🔴 What the second value is: life expectancy, an actuarial note, a number with
  no label. Undecided.

## Phase 4 — SEX · Ch. 4 · 🔒 the "last year" gag

| Beat | |
|---|---|
| Prompt | **SEX:** |
| Intrusive thought | *last year* — **present tense, ungated.** He did not choose it; the system answers a thought he never meant to send |
| System | 🔒 **"Not a date field."** |
| Then | 🔒 He failed to answer, so it **auto-detects and fills it in anyway** |

**Author's draft:** *Sex? / Last Year / Not a date field, defaulting to
biological reading. Male.*

⚖ **One word off: "biological reading" is the system being too careful.** It
hedges — it implies the machine knows a distinction exists and is being precise
about which one it applies. That is a *thoughtful* system, and the governing
rule says it understands nothing. A dumb transcriber would not qualify its own
epistemology; it would **look at the meat and write it down.**

⚖ Proposed, clipped into separate sentences because system text should not
splice: **"Not a date field. Defaulting to scan. Male."** More invasive, funnier,
and in character — the horror is that it resolved the field **by examining him
without asking.**

⚖ Also settle whether the prompt is `SEX:` (a label, like the other fields) or
*"Sex?"* (a question). The label is colder and sets the intrusive thought up
better; the question is funnier as a straight line. Either is fine — be
consistent with Name and Age.

⚙️ Land it at the worst possible moment of the sprint. ⚖ If the crowd is ever
shown reacting, here is where they first laugh.

## Phase 5 — SPONSOR · Ch. 4 · **the real one**

**Tonal turn.** The first four fields were comedy. This one is invasive, and
the prose should stop being funny without announcing that it has.

1. **He has no idea.** Genuine blank — he does not know he has a sponsor.
2. **The scour.** ⚖ It surfaces memories in the wrong order at the wrong weight
   — trivia at full sensory vividness — because it does not rank, it retrieves.
3. 🔒 **THE MEMORY SEQUENCE.** Marcus **fired** · **drunk, in his pajamas** ·
   sees **Paul, a homeless man** · **helps him without knowing him** · is given
   **the ticket** in return · **pockets it without thinking, crumpling it.**
   Paul keeps the bible's blindness, skeletal frame and **crumpled expensive
   suit** — on a homeless man the suit finally explains itself.
   ⚠️ He gave the ticket *"from a friend"* he would not name.

   🎯 **DO NOT LET THE SCOUR TELL A STORY.** The system does not rank or
   sequence — it dumps a **search result**. Surface the fragments in a useless
   order (the pocket first, the firing last) at flat, wrong-weighted vividness,
   and let **the reader** assemble what Marcus does not. That keeps the beat
   short inside a chase, it is the governing rule working, and it rewards a
   re-read. A narrated flashback here will stall Ch. 4 dead.
4. 🔒🔒 **THE RESOLUTION, DISPLAYED AS A FAULT.** The field populates **PAUL**,
   flickers, errors, and settles on **PLUTUS**. The crowd reads a transcription
   fumble. **It is correct.**

### Why the glitch is the best beat in the arc

- ⭐ **The irony inverts.** Until now the device has given the *reader* what
  *Marcus* lacks. Here the reader gets what **the in-world audience** lacks —
  the spectators, who are supposed to hold every advantage, get it wrong.
- ⭐ **It plants the reveal by saying it out loud.** Canon lists *Paul reads as
  Plutus* as an inference to confirm. This confirms it in text and hands
  everyone a reason to dismiss it. There is no better way to plant a reveal.
- ⭐ **The alibi is airtight and free: both names start with P.** A phonetic
  transcriber mangling *Paul* into *Plutus* is entirely plausible in-world.
- ⭐ **It hides the single most important fact in the book.** Paul gave the
  ticket *"from a friend"* — so if Paul is Plutus, he handed over his own ticket
  pretending to be a courier. The field exposes that lie for one frame, and the
  glitch buries it again.
- ⭐ **It mispriced the odds.** A crowd that believes his sponsor is a rendering
  error is betting on a man whose sponsor is real. Marcus is **undervalued
  because of a display bug** — a casino story where the house has bad
  information on its own board.
- ⭐ **It is thematically exact for Plutus specifically**, who is blind in canon
  and blind in myth. A god who cannot be seen properly is rendered by a system
  that cannot see him properly.

🎯 **Craft instruction: DO NOT WINK.** No narratorial nudge, no *odd, that*. He
does not notice because he has no reason to think either name matters. State it
and move on.

6. 🔒 **THE BURN.** The ticket is in his sweatpants pocket — the same gray
   sweatpants the broadcast showed the whole planet in Ch. 1. It ignites and
   brands the thigh, **burning a hole through the pants that the audience can
   see through.**
   - 🔒 **The brand is a CRUMPLE PATTERN**, because he crumpled the ticket
     pocketing it. It reads as damage, not as a mark — ⭐ the same joke as the
     sponsor glitch, in flesh. *The true thing arrives looking like a
     malfunction.*
   - ⚖ Flash paper burns fast and clean, so the hole has a scorched edge and
     the crumple shows through it — which makes the misread even easier. It
     looks like he was hit by something.
   - ⚖ **He feels it a beat late.** Present: the pocket is burning. Past: he
     registered his leg was on fire. The tense rule delivered as physical
     sensation — his best chance to teach the device through the body.
   - 🔒 **The thigh brand SUPERSEDES the bible's** *"golden rectangle on the
     chest."* One mark, not two. ⚠️ It is a **brand**, never a *tattoo*.
   - ✅ **The visible-mark question is closed by the hole** — the mark makes its
     own window, so the show needs no added livery. ⭐ Involuntary exposure, and
     mundanely shameful: a hole in your sweatpants at thigh height is not a
     heroic scar in a heroic place. He wears it until he finds other clothes.
   - ⚖ **He sees the hole before he understands the brand** — a hole appears in
     his pants while he is running (present); he registers what made it
     afterwards (past).

## Phase 6 — COMPLETION AND THE LOCK · Ch. 5

- Registration completes at the shelter door.
- The HUD resolves. **Patron: Plutus.** And the tab is **locked.**
- ⭐ One sentence, and the Forsaken cruelty arrives as a **UI state**: he has
  been assigned a god he cannot reach. The bible's HUD spec already greys the
  patron icon — this is that detail paying off.
- ⚖ If the odds (bible: **1:259**) appear anywhere, here. Mispriced, per Phase 5.

## Phase 7 — THE BUTTON · Ch. 5

- Teleport. 🔒 **"Welcome, sugar~!"**
- ⭐ The tilde matches *"Found you~!"* from Ch. 1 — so **the reader identifies
  Momus a beat before Marcus does.** The tense device operating at arc scale.
- 🔴 **THE SISTER.** He never asks. The system offers no field for her and he
  never thinks to request one. ⚖ Let the completion summary list everything he
  entered, with nothing about anyone else on it — **and let him not notice.**
  That absence is the arc's real ending, and it costs nothing to stage.
