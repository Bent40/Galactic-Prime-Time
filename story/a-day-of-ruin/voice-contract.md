# Voice contract — *A Day of Ruin*

**Read this before any line edit, copy edit, or prose review of this novel.**
It exists because the rules below look like errors and are not. A reviewer who
edits without it will "correct" the book's central device. That has already
happened once (2026-09-22) and is the reason this file exists.

Status: author-declared. Anything marked ⚖ is an open question, not a rule.

---

## POV and person

First person, single narrator — **Marcus ("Mark")**. No head-hopping.

## Register

Sardonic deadpan inner monologue. Crude profanity is **in voice**, not
excess. The signature structure is a **deliberate slow-mundane build to an
inconvenienced punchline** — the narrator meets an apocalypse with the
irritation of a man whose Saturday was interrupted, and keeps doing so past
the point where it is reasonable.

Cynical, satirical, bleak. Per the series bible: no hero power fantasy, no
divine redemption, no chosen one.

---

## 🔒 THE TENSE SYSTEM — the deliberate rule-break

**The narration is thought as it arrives.** Tense is therefore not a timeline,
it is a **perception gate**:

| Tense | Means |
|---|---|
| **Past** | The narrator has **registered** this. It has been processed. (immediate past) |
| **Present** | This is **happening without him registering it.** (immediate present) |

So mixed tense inside a paragraph — even inside a sentence — is the system
working. **Do not normalize it.** Flag only where the rule reads *ambiguously*
(a clause whose gate cannot be determined), never the mixing itself.

### Worked examples from Ch. 1

- *"**I look** back at my sister. ... **I took** a minute to look at her."* —
  reflex glance (ungated, present) then deliberate attention (registered,
  past). Two sentences, same object; the tense is the difference between
  seeing and *looking*.
- The whole broadcast runs in **present** while he dismisses it as CGI —
  *"the caster's neck twists," "his skin glows," "On the screen appear 2
  photos"* — then flips to **past** at the instant of cognition: *"The second
  photo **was** much more familiar"* → *"That guy **was** me."*
  **The tense performs the reveal before the sentence does.**
- *"My sister did not finish that sentence, because I **had already** pulled
  her away."* — pluperfect: the body acted and the narration caught up
  afterward. The rule pushed one step further than stated.
- *"his glasses fell crooked on his nose ... considering **I** haven't worn my
  glasses"* — the pronoun walks third → first inside one sentence at the moment
  he stops reading a stranger's description. Same machine. Leave it.

### Consequences to write toward

- **Present tense is a dread instrument.** Once the reader has the rule,
  present tense means *something is happening to Marcus that he has not
  noticed.* Use it deliberately; do not spend it on texture.
- **It is also a progression bar.** As he gets better at surviving, the
  present-tense passages should shrink on their own. In a progression-genre
  serial this is a re-read mechanic. ⚖ Not yet committed to as a plan.
- **Intrusive thoughts are ungated by definition** — they arrive in present,
  he did not choose them, and anything that answers *them* rather than him is
  answering something he never meant to send. (This is the engine for the
  registration-scene comedy; the humour and the prose system are the same
  machine.)

### ⚖ Open

Whether the rule should be **legible on first read** (sharpen the first
present-tense intrusion so a reader clocks it early) or **retroactive only**
(it resolves at *"That guy was me"*, and early serial reviewers will call it a
tense error for a few chapters). Undecided.

## Things that are not errors

- Sentence fragments used as beats (*"Terrified, that is."*)
- Profanity in narration and dialogue
- The mundane opening — it is load-bearing, not slack
- Present-tense habitual asides about the house and the sister

## Things that are errors

Ordinary typos, dialogue punctuation, and doubled words. These get fixed
normally. See the review log in `../README.md` for what has been run.
