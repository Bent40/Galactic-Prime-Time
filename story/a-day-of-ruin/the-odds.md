# The odds — the interaction economy

🔒 **RULED 2026-09-22 (author).** A standing mechanic for the whole novel, not a
scene detail. Markers: 🔒 ruled · ⚖ proposed, unblessed · 🔴 open.

---

## The rule

🔒 **Betting on a contestant, and reaching one — goals, contact, direction —
costs divinity, and the price scales with the ODDS.**

🔒 **Marcus's odds are so skewed that reaching him is prohibitively
expensive.** Consequences:

- 🔒 **Every goal he receives is COLLECTIVE.** Never one god — a pool. No
  individual can carry the cost alone.
- 🔒 **So his goals arrive rarely.** Long gaps, and the gaps are the norm.
- 🔒 **He does not know how many are watching, or how many are betting for or
  against him.**

## 🔒 The reveal

**He walks out to an applauding crowd after his table is done.** That is the
payoff, held for as long as it takes.

---

## Why this is load-bearing

### ⭐⭐ It turns "Forsaken" from a rule into a PRICE

Canon defines the Forsaken table as *"no help from the gods at all; everything
solved alone"* — which reads as a prohibition somebody wrote. **This makes it
economics.** Nobody is forbidding help. **Helping Marcus simply costs more than
he is worth.**

Strictly better, three ways:

- The cruelty needs no villain. **The market does it**, which is exactly
  canon's register — the gods are bored and petty, never mustache-twirling.
- It is consistent with Plutus going all-in: a god nobody else will spend on is
  precisely the bet a desperate god makes.
- ⭐ **The isolation can end without a rule changing.**

### ⭐⭐ Therefore: HIS ODDS ARE THE PROGRESSION BAR

Not levels, not stats — **how expensive he is to talk to.** As **1:259**
narrows, gods can start to afford him: goals come more often, a single patron
becomes possible at all, the silence thins.

**The reader watches his isolation lift as a number.** That is a progression
mechanic made entirely out of the setting's own economy, in a genre where the
progression axis is usually bolted on. It is already implied by the rule above;
it only needs to be *used*.

### ⭐ It gives the silence a second cause

Enki goes quiet at the teleport, ruled as impartiality. Under this economy it
may **also** be that nobody can afford to speak. Two explanations for one
silence — ⭐ and Marcus can eventually learn the cheap one and never the true
one.

### ⭐ Collective goals should read as COMMITTEE WORK

A pooled goal was negotiated. So his rare goals should feel **vague,
compromised, oddly worded** — a corporate mission statement rather than a
command. ⚖ And the shape of a goal is diagnostic: it says something about who
was in the pool, if he ever learns to read it.

### 🔴 The one canon interaction to settle

⚠️ Canon: *"**Tipping the dealer:** at **every non-Forsaken table**, gods can tip
the dealer to help their luck — boons, buffs, items…"* — so tipping is barred at
Marcus's table outright, not merely priced.

⚖ **Recommended reading, which preserves canon exactly: A GOAL IS NOT HELP.**
Tipping (boons, buffs, items) stays flatly barred at a Forsaken table. **Goals
are a different instrument** — content direction, the show telling a contestant
what to do. Not aid, and frequently the opposite.

⭐ **Which gives a better line than the workaround deserves: the only thing the
crowd can afford to send him is WORK.** Never a gift. Instructions. And he
cannot refuse, argue with, or even identify the source of a demand that arrives
from nowhere every few weeks.

## 🟡 THE SWING — proposed 2026-09-22, the moment the economy is created

**Normal odds show on him first.** Then the table type is picked — and the
odds **swing immensely against him in one beat**, as everyone collectively
predicts his death and, in doing so, **locks themselves out of ever helping
him.**

### ⭐⭐ The lock-out is the whole idea, and it cuts both ways

**Nobody decides to abandon him.** Every god makes an individually sensible
bet — *he is going to die, bet accordingly* — and **in aggregate, in about four
seconds, they price him out of all contact.** The isolation is not a rule, not
a punishment and not a house decision. It is **a crowd being rational, once.**

⭐ **And the against-bettors lock themselves out too.** Canon bars tipping at a
Forsaken table in both directions — no boons, and no *"trials, monsters,
curses"* either. So the people who bet on his death **cannot arrange it.**
Everyone who moved is now a pure spectator. **The swing does not just isolate
Marcus; it converts the entire audience into an audience.**

⭐⭐ **Which retro-explains why Forsaken pays so much.** Canon says the format
carries *"much higher divinity… the sponsoring god's payout is much higher"*
and never says why. Now it does: **a table nobody can touch is the only honest
bet in the casino.** The odds cannot be manipulated, so the payout is real.
Falls straight out of the beat; nothing needs inventing.

### 🔴 Who picks the table — canon has an answer

⚠️ Canon is explicit and recent: *"Forsaken games are **god-initiated**… the
champion is chosen by the gods… **never the champion's refusal of patronage.**
(Marcus: Plutus went all-in; **consent not included.**)"*

So a real choice by Marcus contradicts it. ⚖ **Recommended third option, which
is funnier than either and costs no canon: the choice is OFFERED AND IS NOT
ONE.**

⭐⭐ **Because the SPONSOR field already decided it.** He filled that in two
fields ago. **The form asks him a question it has already answered** — which is
the tired-oracle bureaucracy, *"consent not included"*, and the register of the
whole book, in one gesture. The brand on his thigh is the contract; he has been
carrying it since before the aliens arrived.

⚖ Available but note the cost: **Marcus genuinely picking Forsaken out of
ignorance** is tempting, and it makes him complicit in a way that weakens him
as Plutus's victim — and it breaks *"never the champion's refusal."*

### 🔒 THE NUMBER — do not inflate it

**The author's instinct is correct.** Three reasons, the second decisive:

1. **Past about 1:100 the number stops being felt.** 1:259 and 1:25,000 produce
   the same reaction — *very bad.* Inflation buys no emotion.
2. ⭐⭐ **It would break the progression bar ruled above.** If his odds ARE the
   axis, they need a **legible range**. 259 → 180 → 90 is a story a reader can
   feel. 25,000 → 24,300 is noise. **Inflating the number destroys the mechanic
   it was invented to dramatise.**
3. **The drama is the MOVEMENT, not the magnitude.** *"Biggest turnover ever"*
   is a **delta**. Maximise the contrast and the speed, not the endpoint.

⭐ **Land it on canon's 1:259 and change nothing.** It is already the opening
figure, and it is *unround* — **a crowd that prices a man at 1:259 has done
sums.** 1:10,000 reads as contempt; **1:259 reads as appraisal**, which is
colder. They did not dismiss him. They **evaluated** him.

**What to maximise instead:**

- ⭐ **How ordinary the BEFORE number is.** Make it forgettable — even money,
  or mildly unfavourable. The more mundane the before, the more violent the
  after, and the reader can hold a baseline.
- ⭐ **Speed.** One re-render, not a climbing ticker. Instant collective
  judgment.
- ⭐⭐ **Put the big number on the COUNT, not the ratio.** *How many wagers just
  landed* scales as far as wanted without breaking anything, and it is the
  figure that actually describes the abandonment. **Two numbers, two jobs:** the
  ratio stays legible, the crowd size gets to be horrifying.
- ⭐ **The oracle does not react.** The largest turnover in the casino's history
  renders, and the clerk moves to the next field. *That* is the horror.

## ⚠️ Authorial discipline — what protects the reveal

🔒 **Marcus must never be able to tell rarity from absence.** He has no way to
distinguish *"the pool could not afford it"* from *"nobody is watching."* That
ambiguity is the entire engine.

⚠️ **So the narration must not explain the economics — to him OR to the
reader.** If the reader works out that silence means *expensive* rather than
*empty*, the applause becomes a confirmation instead of a reversal. **Let the
reader share his conclusion and be wrong with him.** Explain afterwards, or
never.

## ⭐⭐ Why the applause is the right ending for this arc

- **It is the tense device at book scale.** The crowd is the one thing *always
  present and never registered* — in every scene as a number, processed in
  none. The applause is the moment it finally registers. **The book's smallest
  device and its largest reveal are the same mechanism.**
- **The odds were standing in for the crowd the whole time**, which is what the
  casino thinks a crowd is. The reveal converts arithmetic back into people.
- ⚠️ **And it should feel good and be horrible.** They are applauding a man who
  spent the entire table believing nobody was there — and under canon's rule
  that the gods are *"addicted, bored, starving for spectacle,"* **the isolation
  was the content.** They enjoyed it. He should not be able to separate the
  warmth from the proof.
